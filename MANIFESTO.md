# Dev Doodles Manifesto

> This is a stalker's notebook from the Zone of the Linux kernel.
>
> I do not map the Zone.
> I only record the paths I actually walked and the anomalies worth revisiting.

## The Stalker's Code

- **A small experiment beats a large plan.**
  A 50-line program can illuminate a thousand pages of documentation.

- **One doodle, one idea.**
  Every experiment exists to internalize exactly one new concept. Scope is the enemy.

- **Man pages are the asylum orderlies.**
  Experiments for their own sake should be avoided. If something is already clear from the documentation, move on.

- **Reality wins over bold assumptions.**
  If an observation contradicts my mental model, the observation wins.

- **Notes are first-class citizens.**
  These notes may be incomplete, naive, or wrong. They only need to be useful to my future self.

- **Roots before branches.**
  I study mechanisms that outlive technologies: memory, synchronization, protocols, logic, operating systems, compilers, hardware. Frameworks and products can wait until work requires them.

- **Today's abstraction boundary is sacred.**
  A flip-flop does not require learning transistor physics. Sockets do not require implementing Ethernet. Descend one layer only after finishing the current one.

- **Automation is a trap.**
  Commenting lines of code is enough. No dashboards, no elaborate note-taking systems, no productivity theater.

- **Bugs are part of the landscape.**
  I fix them when I encounter them. Some remain buried forever. That's acceptable.

- **Premature refactoring is the actual smell.**
  A doodle is disposable. It is a microscope, not a cathedral.

- **Finished, not expanded.**
  The repository grows by completing experiments, not by endlessly improving existing ones.

- **Work pays for work.**
  Employer-specific technologies belong to working hours whenever possible. Personal time is reserved for foundations.

- **Curiosity over anxiety.**
  I do not study technologies because job descriptions frightened me. I study mechanisms because understanding compounds.

- **Comparison is background noise.**
  There will always be someone younger, faster, or better at interviews. My only benchmark is whether today's mental model is better than yesterday's.

- **The purpose is not to become a Linux guru.**
  The purpose is to remain a humble, curious engineer.

Welcome to the Zone, stalker.
